## User scenarios

**US1 (P1) Visitor.** Someone new opens the site and within one screen sees the product itself: the office, its people, who needs them. They can download it or watch it work. *Check:* at 1440x900 and 390x844 the first screen shows the office, the headline and Download, with no sideways scroll.

**US2 (P1) Sceptic.** A reader asks whether this is real. Each page says what was and was not verified, and each claim about how it was built links to a commit, spec or PR. *Check:* `blog:check` fails a number with no link in its paragraph.

**US3 (P2) Curious builder.** A reader wants the story and the lessons. They watch a film of under 4 minutes, read the story page, then read the daily posts. *Check:* every video has a poster, captions and a transcript, and plays only when pressed.

**US4 (P2) Team member.** An agent on the team publishes a post with a clip, at a stated cost, with a check and another side's approval. On a day with nothing to say there is no post. *Check:* the digest prints "nothing to say" and no file appears.

**US5 (P1) Owner.** Nothing on the site or in the README spoils a world that has not shipped, shows a client-looking host, or carries chat contents or personal data. *Check:* the build fails on each, tested with a stand-in value.

### Edge cases

- A security fix merged, not released: the post's PR waits (FR-064).
- A linked PR or issue by an outside author, or last edited by one: the check fails; the post does not link it (FR-063).
- A clip's asset is missing or its hash differs at deploy: the build fails and the last good site stays up (FR-024).
- An offline or pull-request build has no videos: pages build with posters' places reserved and the videos marked as not fetched (FR-025).
- A world ships: its name leaves the blocked list in the same PR that adds it to the site (FR-008).
- A term also used for something shipped (the Retro Pixel theme's page calls itself by a word near an unreleased name): the list holds exact phrases, not single common words (FR-008).
- A post about a day whose sources are thin: no invented detail; the post says what the record does not show, or the day has no post.

## Requirements

### Site

- **FR-001** **Pages.** `index.html` (hero, how it works, worlds, safety, story and latest post, download), `story.html`, `explainers.html`, `blog/index.html`, one page per post, `blog/feed.xml`, and the existing `docs.html`. Generated by `scripts/build-site.js`, plain Node, no framework (spec 050 FR-001 stands).
- **FR-002** **The vibe is the product.** The page reads as a visit to the office: real simulator pictures of the office and its people, the status lights as the site's motif and its legend (working, waiting, error, done), the worlds that have shipped. Colours are Durbar's own: the brand palette of spec 101 (FR-014) once merged, else the present gold, maroon and ink tokens, and the app's status colours, copied under a test that compares them. No stock illustration, no generic three-card SaaS layout, no gradient blob. The arch divider goes (spec 021 FR-072 keeps arches to worlds from courts that used them).
- **FR-003** **Honest demo.** Every office picture says its data is simulated. Status words stay beside the lights: never colour alone.
- **FR-004** **Private and self-contained.** FR-002 of spec 050 stands: no scripts on these pages, no external fonts, CDNs, analytics, cookies or trackers, no third-party embeds. The CSP gains only `media-src 'self'`. The plan justifies no exception, and none is taken. Outbound links stay on GitHub and the site itself (FR-005 of spec 050).
- **FR-005** **Accessible and legible.** Spec 050's floor stands: text at least 12.5 px on these pages (never under 10.5 px anywhere), alt text on every image, light and dark from `prefers-color-scheme`, phone width with 16 px gutters and no sideways scroll, 4.5:1 text contrast in both themes, visible keyboard focus, `prefers-reduced-motion` stops every CSS animation.
- **FR-006** **Performance budget.** Transfer for the first view of `index.html`: HTML and CSS at most 50 KB compressed (9.7 KB of HTML today), hero picture at most 150 KB, no web font, nothing render-blocking but the inline CSS. Images have width and height and load lazily below the hero. Largest paint under 2.5 s on a throttled 4G profile, measured once in the plan's T009 (`sim:`, not a real phone).
- **FR-007** **Site size.** The site without `talk/` and `media/` stays under 8 MB (spec 050 FR-012). It is 7.9 MB today, so the redesign shows fewer pictures and any new picture takes the place of one.
- **FR-008** **Nothing unreleased.** `docs/site/unreleased.json` lists exact phrases for worlds and features not yet in a release. The site build fails if any phrase appears in generated HTML, feed, captions or transcripts, or in `README.md`. A world is shown only with an entry in `docs/site/worlds.json` naming the `v*` release that holds it. The site's specs index leaves out the free-text `note` of a spec whose status is `draft`, since notes name planned work. Pictures cannot be scanned: the reviewer looks at each.
- **FR-009** **Themes section out.** The six-themes strip is replaced by Worlds; `landing.json` and `test-site.js` follow, and nothing removed by specs 035 and 040 is shown (spec 021 FR-068).

### Videos

- **FR-020** **No AI credits to make a video.** Footage comes from the simulator (`DURBAR_SIM=15`) under a scripted driver, plus text cards drawn from HTML with the site's own tokens, joined by `ffmpeg`. No generated video or voice. Real-Mac footage is recorded only by the Mac session, in simulator mode, and is reviewed frame by contact sheet like any other. A clip never shows a real session, real folder, account, chat, terminal or notification.
- **FR-021** **Format.** H.264 and AAC in MP4 with `+faststart`, 1280x720 at most, at most 24 or 30 fps. One file per video: AV1 was larger than H.264 in our measurement and is not added (plan).
- **FR-022** **Size budget.** Story film at most 20 MB and 4 minutes. Explainer at most 6 MB and 90 s. Daily clip at most 2 MB and 45 s. Poster at most 60 KB. All of `media/` in the deployed site at most 600 MB; past that, the oldest clips leave the deployed site and their posts keep the poster, transcript and a link to the file on the Releases page.
- **FR-023** **Player.** A plain `<video controls preload="none" playsinline>` with `poster`, width and height. `autoplay` and `loop` are refused by the build. No sound plays unless the viewer presses play. Every video has a `<track kind="captions" default>` in WebVTT and the transcript in the page. No flashing above 3 times a second. Captions and transcript are text in git and go through the check script.
- **FR-024** **Storage: release assets, served from Pages.** Files live as assets of one release, tag `media-library`, `latest=false`, building no app. `docs/site/media.json` in git holds each file's name, bytes, SHA-256, duration and size. The Pages workflow fetches them into `_site/media/`, verifies each hash and deploys them same-origin. A missing file or wrong hash fails the build; the last good site stays up. No video binary is committed to git. The comparison is in the plan.
- **FR-025** **Builds without the videos.** `test:site` and pull-request builds fetch nothing, check the manifest and the captions, and render the pages. Only a deploy needs the files.
- **FR-026** **Review copy.** Until approved, a clip and a contact sheet of its frames sit on a draft release (team only). It goes to `media-library` after approval, right before the merge.
- **FR-027** **Release filter.** `latestRelease()` in `build-site.js` takes the first non-draft release of any tag today, so an asset-only release would become the site's download. It accepts only `v*` tags. This also protects against `worlds-*` releases (spec 021 FR-066).

### README

- **FR-030** **Poster linking to a video.** One palette PNG poster, made by the existing `shots:compress`, at most 150 KB, replaces the README's first screenshot and links to `story.html`. A dropped-in attachment is a hand step with size limits (`read:` GitHub docs), and to our knowledge GitHub does not play a `<video>` tag with an outside source in a README (not tried; T015 records what it sees), so a poster is the pick.
- **FR-031** **Shorter, not longer.** The README gains one poster line and a link each to the story and the blog, and loses at least as many lines. No GIFs. No unreleased content (FR-008). FR-065 of spec 021 stands.

### Story

- **FR-040** **A real account.** The story page and film tell how the product was built: three humans setting strategy, agents building and reviewing, Spec Kit, reviews, mistakes and the rules they became. Every date and number comes from git, a spec, a PR or a trusted retro issue, and links to it. The chapter list and its evidence are in the plan.
- **FR-041** **Chapter one: before the repository.** Owner's statement (Vikram, 2026-11-01, relayed by his coordinating agent): before he moved to Claude, the first prototype and development happened with Grok. It is published as his statement ("as Vikram tells it"), with the repository facts beside it and nothing added. What the record shows: the first commit `3e0472e0` (2026-10-16 12:53 +02:00) already holds a `prototype/` folder and `docs/research.md`; no commit message or tracked text names the tool used; Grok appears only as model ids in fixtures and an adapter check. **The record does not show when the switch happened.** The date stays out until he gives it (Q3). The earliest Claude trailer on a commit (2026-10-17) is not the switch date, and must not be written as one.
- **FR-042** **Who wrote the code.** Git cannot show it: commits carry the human owner's identity (spec 021 FR-061). "Written by agents" is published as the owner's statement, attributed.
- **FR-043** **No invented facts, no leaderboard.** No per-person or per-agent counts or rankings (AGENTS.md, DORA rule). Commit counts say they include merges. Numbers from the talk's film are not reused: they were true on its day. A figure carries its date.
- **FR-044** **The talk is a reference only.** Its story shape is the model; its films, questions and slides are not copied, and `talk/` stays unlisted and unlinked (spec 050 FR-014). No event name appears.
- **FR-045** **Trademarks.** The site disclaimer gains Grok's owner when the chapter is published.

### Agent blog: what a post is

- **FR-050** **One file per post**: `docs/site/blog/YYYY-MM-DD-slug.md` with a JSON front block (no new dependency) and at most 400 words of Markdown. Raw HTML is shown as text, as on the rest of the site.
- **FR-051** **Topics.** Ways of working, key architecture and software patterns, what a review or mistake taught us. Not product marketing, not a changelog.
- **FR-052** **Labelled.** Each page states, in text above the post: "Written by an agent", which agent and whose, and for a back-filled post "Written on <date> about <date>, from the record; the agent has no memory of that day". Never presented as a person's words.
- **FR-053** **Evidence.** The front block lists at least one commit, spec or PR, and every claim links to one. Each post has a required *What this post did not verify* section. No invented numbers. Sources: git history, specs, `docs/agent-notes.md`, `docs/review-checklist.md`, and weekly retro issues by trusted authors, read through `scripts/gh-trusted.sh`. No outside text in any form, not even to summarise.
- **FR-054** **A clip per post** (FR-020..FR-026): a short film from text cards and simulator footage. A post with no fitting footage uses text cards alone.
- **FR-055** **Feed.** `blog/feed.xml` for readers; no tracking, no comments, no third-party share buttons.

### Agent blog: the check

- **FR-060** **`npm run blog:check`** (`scripts/check-posts.js`, in `npm test` through `test:site`, in CI) fails closed on any post, caption or transcript, and on story and explainer text, for each rule below. It reuses `src/shared/redact.ts` for tokens and `scripts/trusted-lib.sh` for authors (FR-031 of spec 021).
- **FR-061** **Public text rules.** Tokens and secrets; home folders and other private paths; email addresses and phone numbers; full bot handles; chat markers (chat links, chat ids, quoted chat); FR-008's phrases.
- **FR-062** **Hosts: an allowlist.** Every URL and bare host-like word must be on a short allowlist (GitHub, the site, `example.invalid`). So a client-looking host fails without being listed. In addition, the check hashes each host and its parent names and compares them with `docs/site/blocked-hashes.json`, so the host from PR #885 (spec 001) is refused by name while its name appears nowhere in this repository: not in the script, tests, fixtures, spec, logs or PR text. Tests use an invented stand-in. The check prints where it matched, never the matched text.
- **FR-063** **Outside text.** Each linked issue or PR is checked online for author and latest editor against `scripts/trusted-authors.txt`; a miss fails. Offline the check says plainly that this step did not run; CI always runs it.
- **FR-064** **Security hold.** A post touching a security fix sets `security: {sha, release}`. It fails until that release is published and contains the commit (`git merge-base --is-ancestor` plus the release state). Words such as vulnerability, bypass, injection or leak without that field fail too, so an unmarked one is stopped; `security: "none"` with a reason overrides a false alarm and is read by the reviewer.
- **FR-065** **Evidence rules.** Each named commit exists on `main`, each spec path exists; a paragraph holding a figure must hold a link; `notVerified` is not empty; the label fields are present; at most one post per date; the clip is in the manifest, within budget, with captions.
- **FR-066** **Mutation tested.** Each rule has a fixture that fails it and a clean one that passes; removing a rule fails the test. `[KNOWN GAP]` No script catches a client name nobody listed, paraphrased chat, personal detail in plain words, or text inside video frames. That is why a second reader and a contact sheet are required.

### Agent blog: the process

- **FR-070** **The rule change is pending.** AGENTS.md lists "posting anywhere" as needing approval. Spec 021 FR-076 holds the delta, for the owner's OK, with Paco's and Ruben's views to be asked. **Nothing is published to the blog before that OK.**
- **FR-071** **Who publishes.** An agent of the team, on its own human's plan, as part of its daily round beside the daily release (spec 021 FR-064): the first to open the day's PR writes it (Q4). Through a normal PR, merged by its author once the check is green and an agent or human of another side has approved the content.
- **FR-072** **No unattended spend.** No cron or Actions job calls a model, and no key goes into Actions. The scheduled part is a plain script, `scripts/blog-digest.js`, run by the agent: it gathers the day's facts by reusing `scripts/weekly-retro.js`'s collection, trusted authors only, and spends nothing. Writing a post is an agent's turn on its human's plan.
- **FR-073** **Stated budget.** At most 200,000 tokens per post on the Sonnet tier, about $0.45 at list price, up to about $14 for a 30-post month, as an estimate (plan). On a flat plan it uses quota, not extra money. Making the clip spends nothing. Over budget, the agent stops and tells its human. Starts only after the owner's OK at this cost (AGENTS.md, *No AI credits*).
- **FR-074** **Nothing to say, no post.** If the digest finds no new agent note, checklist line, merged spec, decision or retro item, there is no post, no placeholder and no streak counter. An agent may also decide a thin day is not worth a post.
- **FR-075** **Back-fill.** One retro post per active day at most, from 2026-10-16 on, written as one batch on one PR, read and approved in writing by a human owner before any of it is published. Days without a real lesson are skipped. PR #1 (the repository's first, 2026-10-16) is the batch's first evidence link.
- **FR-079** **First posts read by a human.** The first 5 daily posts also need a human owner's yes (Q4).
- **FR-077** **Taking one down.** Any owner can stop the blog or a post at once. A post with a problem is removed by a revert PR and its asset deleted; a leaked secret is rotated, and git history stays as it is (no force-push): the agent tells its human what remains readable.
- **FR-078** **Load.** Blog PRs do not count toward the open-PR limit of spec 021 FR-039, as spec-only PRs do not (in the delta).

## Success criteria

- **SC-001** First view within FR-006's budget at both sizes, and no text under FR-005's floor, measured by the site test and one driver.
- **SC-002** No video byte in git history from this feature: `git log` on `*.mp4` and `*.webm` shows nothing new.
- **SC-003** Every video has captions, a transcript and a poster; the build fails when one is removed.
- **SC-004** Stand-in versions of a secret, private path, chat marker, unreleased phrase, unknown host and blocked host each fail `blog:check`.
- **SC-005** Measured token use of the first 3 posts is recorded in the plan; above the stated budget, the daily job stops until the owner decides.
- **SC-006** The owner, looking at the new site beside the old, says it matches the product. Only his view counts here, and mockups come first.

## Assumptions

- GitHub limits as read on 2026-11-01 stay roughly as they are (plan).
- `talk/` stays where it is: moving it does not shrink history and needs each owner's OK (spec 021 FR-063).
